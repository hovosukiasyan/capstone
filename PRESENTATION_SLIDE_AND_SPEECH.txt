    • By publication day, the policy window for early action is already closed
    • 11 marzes — 7 years of data (2016–2022) — that's the entire panel
    • No room to just add more data: 7 points per region is a structural limit

  RIGHT BOX — Two Data Assets:
    ① ILCS 2015 Household Survey
       • 5,184 households · 29 features after cleaning
       • Target: total household income (AMD)
       • Key recovered feature: interview month (season matters)

       • 11 marzes · 7 annual observations each (2016–2022)
       • Poverty rate (%), crime rate, hospitals per 100k, population
       • Augmented to monthly frequency for neural model training

WHAT TO SAY (≈ 60 sec):
"Armenia's poverty statistics work like this: ArmStat surveys households, crunches
numbers, and publishes one poverty rate per region, once a year. By the time that
number is public, it's already describing last year. Any policy response is
already late.

My project tries to close that gap with two data sources. First, the ILCS 2015
household survey — over 5,000 households with 29 features after cleaning —
where I build ML models to understand what predicts household income at the
micro level. Second, the ArmStat regional panel covering all 11 marzes from
2016 to 2022 — seven annual observations per region — where I build time-series
models to forecast next year's poverty rate.

The hardest constraint in this project is that seven data points is genuinely
not a lot. Neural networks that normally need hundreds of observations fail
badly here. I had to be honest about that, and let the simplest models win."

SLIDE CONTENT:
  Header: Two-Strand Modelling Pipeline

  LEFT COLUMN — Strand 1: Household ML (ILCS 2015):
    Models: Random Forest · Gradient Boosting · Extra Trees · Ridge · Lasso
    Tuning: Bayesian hyperparameter optimization (Optuna, 50 trials)
    Split:  80% train / 20% test (held out once at the end)
    Best:   GBM + Bayesian Opt → R² = 0.36, MAE = 82K AMD
    Top predictor: income_source_count (r = 0.45)

  RIGHT COLUMN — Strand 2: Regional Forecasting (ArmStat):
    Classical: Lag-1 baseline · Ridge AR · ARIMA
    Neural:    GRU · BiLSTM · Transformer · TCN (PyTorch)
    Honest benchmark: YEARLY (7 real data points per marz)
    Inflated benchmarks: monthly / daily (forward-filled — mechanical)
    Validation: Strict 2022 HOLDOUT — trained 2016–2021 only

  BOTTOM NOTE:
    ★ The 2022 holdout backtest is the only number that counts for regional forecasting.
      All other metrics (monthly R²=0.999) reflect interpolated data, not real signal.

WHAT TO SAY (≈ 60 sec):
"The project has two modelling strands.

The first is a household income model on ILCS 2015. I trained five supervised
regressors and used Bayesian optimization with Optuna to tune the top three.
The best model — Gradient Boosting after tuning — achieves an R-squared of 0.36
on the held-out test set. That's modest, but consistent with the literature on
welfare proxies from single survey waves. The clearest finding from feature
importance: households with diversified income sources are significantly richer.
More income streams, not better assets, is the key predictor.

The second strand is regional time-series forecasting. I benchmarked four neural
architectures against classical baselines at yearly, monthly, and daily
granularity. But I want to be direct: the monthly and daily metrics look
artificially good because I forward-filled annual values to create them. The only
honest benchmark is yearly. And on yearly data, a zero-parameter Lag-1 baseline —
just predicting next year equals this year — beats every neural network. That's
what seven data points buys you.

The real test of all of this is the 2022 holdout backtest. I trained on 2016
through 2021, sealed away 2022, and only looked at it once. That's coming next."

WHAT TO SAY (≈ 60 sec):
"Here is the primary result. Three models, trained exclusively on 2016 to 2021,
evaluated against the actual 2022 poverty rates that ArmStat published.

Lag-1 — the simplest possible model, no parameters whatsoever — achieves a
regional MAE of 5.07 percentage points across all 11 marzes. That's the best
regional accuracy. Ridge AR, which uses lag features and a stress index, does
worse regionally but compensates elsewhere. When you average them 50/50 into
an Ensemble, something interesting happens at the national level: Lag-1 tends to
over-predict recovering regions, Ridge tends to under-predict them, and those
errors cancel under population weighting.

The result: the Ensemble predicts Armenia's 2022 national poverty rate at 24.28%.
The actual number published by ArmStat was 24.3%. That's a 0.02 percentage point
error — effectively zero.

I want to be clear that this is not just lucky. It's the product of a carefully
designed validation setup: trained on one window, tested blind on another. The
methodology is honest, not just the result.

Now let me show you where all of this lives."

WHAT TO SAY — CLOSING (≈ 45 sec):
"Let me close with what I think are the actual takeaways.

First, model complexity does not help when you don't have data. Seven observations
per region means Lag-1 beats GRU and Transformer every time on the honest annual
benchmark. Knowing when to use a simple model is a skill.

Second, income diversification is the single clearest poverty signal in the ILCS
data. More income sources equals systematically higher household welfare —
and that's a finding with real policy relevance.

Third, the validation number that matters — 0.02 percentage points national error
on 2022, trained blind on 2016 through 2021 — is not luck. It's the product of a
careful validation setup and an honest communication of what the numbers mean.

The full system is publicly deployed. The data, code, and paper are all available.
I'm happy to take questions."
